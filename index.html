Part-Time vs Full-Time Creche in Dublin: Choosing the Right Pattern

Weighing up part-time and full-time creche? Compare routine, cost structure, availability, settling in and funding so you can choose the pattern that suits your child and family.

Once you have decided on a creche, the next question is often how many hours your child should attend. Some families need full days, five days a week; others want a few mornings or two or three full days. Many Dublin creches offer a range of options, though availability varies by room and by provider.

There is no right answer for everyone. This guide sets out the main things to consider so you can match your childcare pattern to your work, your budget and, most importantly, your child.

01

What the terms usually mean

Creches use terms such as full-day, part-time and sessional in slightly different ways. Full-day care usually covers most of a working day. Part-time may mean shorter days or fewer days per week. Sessional services offer a short session, often in the morning, and are common for preschool-aged children. Always ask each creche exactly what hours are included in each option.

02

Thinking about your child

Some children settle more quickly with a consistent, daily routine and benefit from seeing the same faces every day. Others enjoy a mix of creche and home time. Age matters too: a baby may cope well with a few days a week, while an older preschooler might be ready for more. Watch how your child responds and be ready to adjust.

  • Does your child thrive on routine or adapt easily to change?
  • How do they handle tiredness after a long day?
  • Would consecutive days help them settle better than scattered ones?
03

Thinking about work and logistics

Your working pattern will often decide the matter. Consider commuting time, drop-off and collection responsibilities, and how much flexibility your employer offers. Remember that creches usually have fixed opening hours and closing days, so build in time for traffic and for days when you are delayed.

04

Cost and funding

Part-time places cost less overall, but the hourly rate can be higher than for full-time places, because creches still need to staff the room. The National Childcare Scheme subsidy is calculated per hour, and the number of subsidised hours can depend on your circumstances. The ECCE free preschool hours also apply in term time for eligible children. Ask each creche for a written breakdown for your exact pattern.

05

Changing pattern later

Your needs may change as your child grows or your work changes. Ask about the creche's policy on increasing or reducing days, the notice period required and whether extra days are available on an ad hoc basis. Some families start part-time and build up; others reduce hours once their child begins ECCE or school.

  • How much notice is needed to change days?
  • Is there a waiting list for extra days?
  • Can we swap days occasionally if needed?
06

Settling in on either pattern

Children on part-time patterns sometimes take a little longer to settle, simply because there are bigger gaps between creche days. If your child attends part-time, consecutive days can help them build familiarity more quickly than days spread across the week. Whatever pattern you choose, a gradual start, short goodbyes and good communication with your child's key person make the early weeks much easier for everyone.

!

Tip: Ask for a trial pattern

If you are unsure, ask whether you can start on one pattern and review it after a few weeks. Many creches are open to a conversation once they see how your child is settling.

Whether you choose full days or a few mornings, the best pattern is one that works for your child and keeps family life manageable.
